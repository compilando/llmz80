AMSTRAD CPC MODE 0 SPRITE

Subject: {prompt}

Mode 0 is the CPC's colour mode, and its pixels are twice as wide as they
are tall on a real screen. That is the single most important thing to know
here: a figure you draw {width} columns wide by {height} rows tall will
appear on the television twice as wide as it looks in the grid below. Draw
it *narrow* -- a standing figure wants roughly half the columns it would
want on a square-pixel machine -- or it will come out squat and fat.

You have four pens, and PENS lists exactly which. Use them for flat areas
that mean something -- body, clothing, a held object -- not for shading a
curve. Four flat colours read; four colours blended do not.

What reads well at this size:
- Bold shapes in flat colour, one pen per body part.
- One accent pen kept for the feature that identifies the character, so the
  eye finds it immediately.
- Deliberate transparency around the figure: the background shows through
  every '.', so the outline is the shape.

What does not work here:
- Drawing at square-pixel proportions and hoping. See above; it is the one
  mistake this mode punishes hardest.
- Checkerboard dithering to fake a fifth colour. At this size it reads as
  dirt, not as shading.
