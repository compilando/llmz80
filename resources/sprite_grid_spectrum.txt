ZX SPECTRUM SPRITE

Subject: {prompt}

This machine draws a sprite as a bitmap: each pixel is either set or it is
not. There is no shading, no dithering into greys and no second colour --
the colour comes later, from the attribute byte Studio derives itself, and
one sprite is one colour across all of it. What you are drawing is a
silhouette, and it lives or dies on being recognisable in solid black at
{width}x{height} pixels.

What reads well at this size:
- A bold, chunky outline with limbs thick enough to survive: a one-pixel
  arm disappears against a busy background on a real screen.
- Big, unambiguous distinguishing features -- a hat, a beak, a weapon held
  clear of the body -- rather than interior detail nobody can resolve.
- Empty space around the figure where the pose allows it, so the shape
  reads against whatever it is standing on.

What does not work here:
- Interior detail: faces, buttons, texture. At sixteen pixels a face is
  three pixels and reads as noise.
- Any attempt at shading, gradient or checkerboard dithering. There is one
  ink colour; a checkerboard is just a hole in the figure.
