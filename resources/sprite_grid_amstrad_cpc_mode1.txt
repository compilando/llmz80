AMSTRAD CPC MODE 1 SPRITE

Subject: {prompt}

Mode 1 is the CPC's everyday mode: square pixels at {width}x{height}, and
four pens. What you draw in the grid is the shape that appears on screen,
proportion for proportion -- unlike mode 0, nothing is stretched.

You have four pens, and PENS lists exactly which. That is enough for a
figure to have a body, a second colour that distinguishes it, and an
accent -- and not enough for shading. Treat each pen as a flat area that
means something.

What reads well at this size:
- A clear silhouette first, colour second: the shape has to be recognisable
  even if every pen were the same.
- One pen reserved for whatever identifies this character, used sparingly
  enough that it stands out.
- Transparency worked into the pose -- gaps between limbs, space under an
  outstretched arm -- so the figure does not read as a block.

What does not work here:
- Outlining the whole figure in one pen and filling with another: at
  sixteen pixels the outline eats the figure.
- Dithering to fake more colours. Four flat pens read; a checkerboard at
  this size reads as noise.
